A 2x2 grid of 4 separate BEARD pieces for a 2D cartoon stick-figure rig. The rig
is a stick figure with a big ROUND head — a plain circle. The piece is pasted on
the lower part of that circle, on top of everything else on the head; the eyes,
brows and mouth are drawn underneath by code and must stay visible. See the
attached reference render for the line weight and the head it has to fit.

Style — match the attached reference exactly:
- Flat vector cartoon, thick uniform black outline with rounded ends, the beard
  as one solid shape with a few interior strand lines.
- EXACTLY TWO COLOURS: black #000000 for the outline and for every interior
  strand line, and one single dark tone #2b2320 for the hair fill. No white
  lines, no grey, no highlights, no gradient, no shading, no third colour.
  A white beard is made later by recolouring this one file — draw the SHAPE of
  a white beard, not the colour.
- Each piece is ONLY the beard hair. NO head, no face, no eyes, no nose, no
  lips, no teeth, no ears, no neck, no skin, no hair on top of the head.
- The two chin pieces (1 and 2) have NO moustache: the strip above the upper lip
  stays empty. The two jaw beards (3 and 4) DO include the moustache, joined to
  the rest so the hair makes one closed ring around the mouth.

Viewing angle — this is a CHEATED FRONT VIEW, not a three-quarter view. Do not
rotate the piece in space and do not add perspective:
- The silhouette stays frontal and symmetric. Both sides are the same width;
  nothing on the far side is foreshortened, narrowed or hidden.
- The CENTRE LINE is shifted to the viewer's RIGHT by about a tenth of the
  piece's width — the tip of a pointed beard, the middle parting of the strands,
  the centre of the mouth gap. It is the shifted centre line that makes the
  character read as facing right.
- All four pieces are turned the same way. Never mirror one of them; facing the
  other direction is done later by flipping the whole character.

Fitting the round head — the part that matters most:
- THE OUTER EDGE IS A CIRCULAR ARC. Every piece is drawn as if traced along the
  rim of one and the same circle, the head. The outline where the beard meets
  the face is a smooth round curve of that circle's curvature — no square jaw,
  no straight chin line, no pointed corners, no wide horseshoe or U shape, no
  cheeks jutting out past the circle.
- THE TOP ENDS STOP AT THE SAME HEIGHT in all four cells, at the widest point of
  that circle. A long beard hangs further down, but where the piece meets the
  face must not move between cells.
- LEAVE A WIDE OPEN MOUTH GAP. The character talks and the mouth is drawn under
  this piece and is animated, so every cell has a large clear opening where the
  mouth goes: at least a third of the head circle's width across and a third of
  its height tall, sitting a little below the middle of the face. The widest
  mouth shape must fit inside that hole with room to spare — err on the side of
  too big. On pieces 3 and 4 the moustache sits high, right under the nose, and
  the hair under the lower lip sits low, so the ring of hair frames the hole
  without ever crowding it. No strand crosses the opening.
- Do NOT fill the lower half of the head solid. A solid mass across the cheeks
  reads as a robber's mask: the cheeks stay open, the hair is a band hugging the
  rim of the circle.
- Every piece centred in its cell with clear margin; nothing touches the sheet
  edges; no grid lines, no labels, no captions.
- Keep relative sizes honest between cells: the short tuft is a visibly smaller,
  thinner shape than the full beard. Do not blow every piece up to fill its cell.

The 4 beards, in this order (left to right, top to bottom):

1. Short chin tuft — an old man's little beard. A small rounded clump of hair on
   the chin only, just under the lower lip, slightly tapered at the bottom.
   Nothing on the jaw, nothing on the cheeks, no moustache.
2. Long pointed sage's beard — the beard of a Vietnamese ông tiên / ông bụt. The
   same chin clump drawn out downward into one long tapering wisp, about twice
   the height of the head, narrow, with a gentle wave and a soft point at the
   end. It hangs from the chin only; the jaw and cheeks stay bare.
3. Light beard, a ring around the mouth only. A narrow band of hair that closes
   in a ring: a moustache high under the nose, running down past both corners of
   the mouth, around under the lower lip and the chin, and back up — one
   continuous shape with a wide hole in the middle where the mouth goes. The
   lower edge follows the circle of the head. The jaw sides and the cheeks stay
   bare.
4. Full jaw beard: the ring of piece 3 plus hair along both sides of the jaw.
   The same closed ring around the mouth, thicker, extended up along both sides
   of the jaw to the widest point of the head circle, and hanging a little below
   the chin. The outer edge is still the arc of that same circle; the cheeks
   above the band stay bare and the mouth hole stays just as wide open.
