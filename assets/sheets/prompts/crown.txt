Style — match the attached reference exactly:
- Flat vector cartoon, thick uniform black outline with rounded ends, the same
  line weight as the stick-figure limbs in the reference.
- EXACTLY TWO COLOURS: black #000000 for the outline and for every interior
  line, and one single dark tone #2b2320 for the whole fill. No gradient, no
  shading, no highlights, no texture, no weave, no pattern, no logo, no brand,
  no third colour. The fill is bleached to white afterwards and recoloured per
  character, so draw the SHAPE of the hat, not its colour. A part that must
  stay dark whatever colour the hat is (a band, a peak, a visor, a badge) is
  painted flat black #000000 — black survives the recolour, any other tone
  does not.
- The piece is ONLY the headwear. NO head, no face, no ears, no hair, no neck,
  no shoulders, no skin. Nothing is worn under it.
- Each piece must be ONE SOLID, FULLY OPAQUE SHAPE. No transparent holes or
  gaps anywhere inside its outline; transparency exists only OUTSIDE the
  outline. The head shows around the piece, never through it.
- THE HAT IS TURNED TO THE VIEWER'S RIGHT, and every piece is turned the same
  way. The character it goes on is looking to the viewer's right; a peak drawn
  head-on is a foreshortened blob and a cap stops reading as a cap. So:
  - The dome — the part that wraps the skull — stays frontal and symmetric, so
    it fits a round head.
  - The FRONT of the hat — peak, bill, brim front, badge, knot, tie — points to
    the viewer's RIGHT and is seen from the side, its full shape visible. The
    back of the hat is on the left.
  - All nine pieces face the same way. Never mirror one of them; facing the
    other direction is done later by flipping the whole character.
  - Tilt is left-right only. Do not draw the hat from above or from below.
- THE HEAD OPENING IS THE SAME IN ALL NINE CELLS. Every piece is drawn as if
  it sits on the same round head: the inner curve where the piece meets the
  skull is the same width in every cell, and its lowest point is at the same
  height in every cell. A brim may be much wider than that opening and a crown
  much taller, but the opening itself must not change size or move.
- The bottom edge is where the piece stops against the head. Close it with a
  clean line — no ragged edge, no hair, no fade.
- Every piece centred in its cell with clear margin; nothing touches the sheet
  edges; no grid lines, no labels, no captions.
- Keep relative sizes honest between cells: a wide-brim hat is a visibly wider
  shape than a scrub cap. Do not blow every piece up to fill its cell.
