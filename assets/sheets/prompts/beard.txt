A 3x2 grid of 6 separate BEARD pieces for a 2D cartoon stick-figure rig. The rig
is a stick figure with a big round head; the piece is pasted on the lower half
of the head, on top of everything else on the head, and the eyes, brows and
mouth are drawn underneath by code. See the attached reference render for the
line weight and the head it has to fit.

Style — match the attached reference sheet exactly:
- Flat vector cartoon, thick uniform black outline with rounded ends, the beard
  as one solid shape with a few interior strand lines.
- EXACTLY TWO COLOURS: black #000000 for the outline and for every interior
  strand line, and one single dark tone #2b2320 for the hair fill. No white
  lines, no grey, no highlights, no gradient, no shading, no third colour.
  A white beard is made later by recolouring this one file — draw the SHAPE of
  a white beard, not the colour.
- Each piece is ONLY the beard hair. NO head, no face, no eyes, no nose, no
  lips, no teeth, no ears, no neck, no skin, no hair on top of the head.
- Front view, straight on, perfectly symmetric. The beard is seen from directly
  in front, both sides equally visible and equally wide — never in
  three-quarter view, never turned, never tilted.
- THE JAW IS THE SAME IN ALL SIX CELLS. Every piece is drawn as if it grows on
  the same round head: the outer curve follows the same jaw line, and the top
  ends of the beard stop at the same height in every cell. A long beard hangs
  further down, but where it meets the face must not move.
- LEAVE A WIDE OPEN MOUTH GAP. Every piece has a large clear opening where the
  mouth is — the character talks, and the mouth is drawn under this piece and
  must never be covered. Make the gap generous, taller and wider than a closed
  mouth; err on the side of too big. This matters most for the two full beards
  (pieces 5 and 6): the hair runs along the jaw and under the lower lip, it
  does NOT close over the mouth.
- Do NOT fill in the whole lower half of the head. A solid mass across the
  cheeks reads as a robber's mask, not a beard: the sides must be a narrow
  strap hugging the jaw, cheeks left open.
- Every piece centred in its cell with clear margin; nothing touches the sheet
  edges; no grid lines, no labels, no captions.
- Keep relative sizes honest between cells: a chin tuft must be a visibly
  smaller, thinner shape than a full beard. Do not blow every piece up to fill
  its cell.

The 6 beards, in this order (left to right, top to bottom):

1. Short chin tuft. A small rounded patch of hair just under the lower lip,
   nothing on the jaw, nothing on the cheeks.
2. Long pointed goatee. The same chin patch drawn out downward into a long
   tapering point, about as long as the head is tall.
3. Santa beard. A big, wide, bushy full beard, rounded at the bottom, hanging
   well below the chin, curling slightly outward at the sides.
4. Chin curtain. Hair along the jaw line and under the chin only, a clear bare
   strip between it and the mouth, and no moustache.
5. Light stubble beard. A thin, even strap of hair following the jaw from
   sideburn to sideburn and a thin band under the lower lip. Cheeks bare.
6. Heavy full beard. The same strap, much thicker, reaching down a head-height
   below the chin, still an open mouth gap and still bare cheeks.
